How scoring works

A quick guide to what “on track” means: how each habit is checked, how a day becomes a percentage, and where the other numbers come from.

1Every habit has a goal

Each habit you track has one simple rule, for example Sleep 7h+ or Stress 5 or lower. When you log a value, the habit lands in one of three states:

  • Met: you logged it and it hit the goal. The cell is filled with the habit's colour.
  • Not met: you logged it but it missed the goal.
  • No data: you haven't logged it that day. The cell stays empty.

2A day's score is the share of goals met

Daily score = habits met ÷ habits you track × 100

Example: you track 5 habits and meet 3 of them. That day scores 3 ÷ 5 = 60%.

  • Every tracked habit is worth the same. {% if personal and habits %}You track {{ habits|length }}, so each one is worth about {{ per_habit }}% of a day.{% else %}With 5 habits, each is worth 20% of a day.{% endif %}
  • A tracked habit you didn't log on a day you did log something counts as not met, so it lowers that day's score.
  • A day with nothing logged for your tracked habits is skipped. It doesn't count as 0%.

3“This week” on the home page

Week % = goals met ÷ (habits you track × days so far this week) × 100

The week runs Monday to Sunday and only counts days that have started. On Wednesday you have had 3 days, so with 5 tracked habits the most you can have met is 15. Meeting 9 of them shows 60%.

The percentage is out of every habit and every day so far, so a day you didn't log pulls it down.

4Dashboard numbers

  • Avg score: the average of your daily scores across all logged days.
  • Best score: your highest single day.
  • 7-day trend: a rolling average of the last 7 logged days, which smooths out one-off good or bad days.
  • Streaks: consecutive days a habit was met. One missed or unlogged day resets the current streak, and the best streak is kept.
  • What moves your score: how strongly each habit rises and falls with your daily score (needs about two weeks of logs). Closer to 1 or −1 means a stronger link. It shows a pattern, not a cause.

5What does and doesn't count

  • Counts: goal habits. These have a target and appear in the weekly grid and heatmap.
  • Doesn't count: context inputs such as free hours, task difficulty, day type and weather. They describe your day and never change your score.
  • Always on: {% for h in habits if h.key in core_keys %}{{ h.name }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% else %}Deep work, Sleep and Stress{% endfor %} stay on because the estimate needs them. You can turn the other habits off in setup.
  • Notes never change scores or streaks.

Turning a habit on or off changes the number of habits you are scored against, so the percentages shift with it.

6Estimated score is a different thing

The Estimated score (0–100) on the home page is an experimental model's guess for a day with the inputs you give it. It learns from simulated data, so treat it as a demo. It is separate from your goal score above and has its own typical-error range.
